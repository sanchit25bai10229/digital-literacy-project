While researching cybercrime in India, what surprised me most was the rapid shift toward highly localized "phishing hubs" that use sophisticated social engineering rather than just technical hacking. I was particularly struck by the scale of "Courier Scams" and "Digital Arrest" frauds, which exploit fear and the authority of law enforcement to bypass modern security software. It revealed that the weakest link in the digital chain isn't always the code, but the human element.

As a result, the one habit I am personally changing is my handling of unsolicited communications. I have moved from a "trust but verify" mindset to a "verify then trust" approach. Specifically, I will no longer click links or provide information to any service—even if the caller ID or message appears legitimate—without first independently contacting the organization through their official, verified website or app. This simple pause creates a critical barrier against the psychological tactics used in modern Indian cybercrime.
